02Two ways to settle, both bad

Expose the trade, or hand a third party all of it.

On a public chain

  • Price, size and both counterparties are readable the moment it settles — often before.
  • For a block, the fact of the trade is the leak.

Off-chain, through an agent

  • The settlement agent is handed the whole trade to confirm one fact.
  • Payment and delivery are separate movements, reconciled afterwards.
Evidence that leakage is real · Tradeweb, “Information leakage in US credit: evidence from all-to-all RFQs”, 2026 (platform-operator research, indicative)

13Why this needs Canton

On a shared-state chain, “the agent shouldn’t see the price” is a request.

On Canton it is a property of the contract.

Visibility is declared in Daml as signatories and observers, and the protocol delivers a contract only to the parties on it. Every trade page lists its transactions, and Verify reads each one back from the node as the signed-in party. A party that was not on the trade is told: this transaction does not exist for you.

17What is not true yet

A prototype, said plainly.

Real

  • Canton Coin and BitSafe CBTC, on DevNet, each through its own registry.
  • Privacy and atomicity, enforced by the ledger and tested against it.
  • Seven live parties on the shared DevNet participant, with on-chain custody accounts.

Not real

  • BOND-2031 and CashUSD are demo assets, not a security or cash.
  • DevNet CC and CBTC have no market value; faucet amounts are tiny.
  • Custodial demo wallet — no end-user signing yet.
  • No legal title transfer, no production custody or compliance.
An external payment lock is defined by its registry: the sender can withdraw it. The seller's exposure is that settlement can fail, not that value is lost.
