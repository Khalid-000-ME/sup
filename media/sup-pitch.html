00Two ways to settle, both bad

Expose the trade, or hand a third party all of it.

On a public chain

  • Price, size and both counterparties are readable the moment it settles.
  • Often before.

Off-chain

  • The settlement agent gets the whole trade to confirm one fact.
  • Payment and delivery are separate movements, reconciled afterwards.

09Why this needs Canton

On a shared-state chain, “the agent shouldn’t see the price” is a request.

On Canton it is a property of the contract.

Visibility is declared in Daml as signatories and observers, and the protocol delivers a contract only to the parties on it. Every trade page lets you read each transaction back as the signed-in party. A party that was not on the trade is told it does not exist.

10The whole flow
Propose
Seller sends private terms — only the named buyer receives them
Accept
Buyer accepts — escrow opens
Lock
Seller locks the asset, buyer locks the payment — each one transaction, now binding
Verify
Agent re-computes one reference — a trade id and a document hash, nothing else
Settle
Either principal settles — both deliveries commit in one transaction, or neither does

12Who it is for

The operations team that already runs a confirmation step.

they already
pay a named third party to confirm every delivery
their clients
treat price and counterparty as confidential
and they carry

Not our user: anyone who needs price discovery. Sup begins after two parties have agreed a price.

13What is not true yet

A prototype, said plainly.

Real

  • Canton Coin and BitSafe CBTC, on DevNet, through their own registries.
  • Privacy and atomicity, enforced by the ledger and tested against it.

Not real

  • The bond and the cash token are demo assets, not securities.
  • Custodial demo wallet, no per-party signing yet.
  • We have not interviewed settlement-operations teams, so we claim no savings.
14Ask

Settlement that proves delivery without exposing the trade.

One introduction: a tokenized-fund administrator or broker that settles institutional trades today, and a settlement agent willing to review exactly what it would see.
